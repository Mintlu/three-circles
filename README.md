# Three Circles 🌱

### An open-source self-discovery game about turning curiosity into real-world experiments.

**Working title: 无聊么？ / Bored?**

> From scrolling → playing → thinking → doing.

Three Circles is an experimental open-source game about discovering what makes life feel meaningful — not by taking a personality test, but by exploring, making choices, collecting clues, and trying small things in the real world.

You don't start by answering:

- What are you passionate about?
- What are you good at?
- What are your values?

Instead, you simply enter a small world.

You explore.

You make choices.

You notice strange things.

And gradually, the game notices patterns too.

---

## 🌱 The Idea

Many self-discovery tools begin with difficult questions:

> “What do you really want?”

But sometimes that's exactly the problem.

We don't know.

And when we're used to scrolling through short-form content, even thinking about a question for a full minute can feel surprisingly difficult.

So Three Circles starts somewhere easier:

> **“Bored? Let's go somewhere.”**

The player explores a cozy world filled with small events, strange objects, characters, and mysteries.

Their choices leave behind clues.

Over time, those clues begin to connect.

---

## 🎮 Core Loop

```text
Explore
   ↓
Discover clues
   ↓
Collect clue creatures
   ↓
Notice recurring patterns
   ↓
Unlock hidden parts of the map
   ↓
Discover a mysterious egg
   ↓
Try a tiny experiment in real life
   ↓
Return and reflect
   ↓
The egg changes...
```

The goal is not to tell the player:

> “This is who you are.”

Instead, the game says:

> **“Something interesting might be here. Want to test it?”**

---

## 🌿 Clue Creatures

Clues are represented as small collectible creatures.

A single choice should never define the player.

A creature becomes more meaningful only when the same pattern appears across unrelated situations.

For example:

**Something unknown appears → Player approaches it**

**A friend has a confusing problem → Player wants to understand it**

**A difficult task appears → Player researches it and tries**

These may gradually reveal a recurring pattern.

But the game doesn't rush to name it.

> **Analysis stays behind the scenes.  
> Discovery happens in front of the player.**

---

## 🗺️ Islands

When several clues begin to connect, unexplored parts of the world appear.

Fog disappears.

A new island becomes visible.

The player doesn't immediately know why.

Exploration itself becomes part of self-discovery.

---

## 🥚 Eggs Are Hypotheses

At the intersection of several recurring clues, the player may discover an egg.

The egg is **not a personality type**.

It represents a possibility.

And it cannot fully hatch inside the game.

The player has to take it into the real world.

Maybe:

- make something tiny,
- help someone solve a real problem,
- try an activity they've been curious about,
- or move an old idea one step closer to reality.

Then they return.

The game asks what happened.

If the experience feels meaningful, the egg may begin to hatch.

If it doesn't?

That's useful information too.

Not every egg has to hatch.

---

## ❤️ ⭐ 🌍 Why “Three Circles”?

Underneath the game are three kinds of evidence:

**What draws you in.**  
❤️ Things you repeatedly move toward.

**How you naturally act.**  
⭐ Patterns in how you explore, solve, create, help, organize, or learn.

**What feels worth doing.**  
🌍 People, problems, and changes that repeatedly matter to you.

The player doesn't need to fill in three circles.

The circles emerge gradually from lived evidence.

Their intersections become **possibilities worth testing**, rather than answers about who the player should become.

---

## 🧠 Interaction Philosophy

Three Circles is designed around a simple principle:

### Tap first. Choose second. Type last.

If the player can interact with something, don't ask a question.

If they can choose, don't make them write.

Only ask an open-ended question when the moment is worth thinking about.

The experience should gradually move from:

> **scrolling → playing → thinking → doing**

The world has boundaries.

The player has no correct answers.

---

## 🤖 Why AI?

The interesting AI problem isn't simply generating dialogue.

Three Circles explores whether AI can:

- understand open-ended player actions,
- detect recurring patterns without over-interpreting individual choices,
- separate motivation from behavior,
- maintain structured clues across many interactions,
- respond naturally without turning the experience into a questionnaire,
- transform emerging patterns into small, testable real-world experiments.

The AI should not act as an authority on who the player is.

It should behave more like a curious companion:

> **“I noticed something. Should we investigate?”**

## 🚧 Current Status

**Early prototype / active experiment.**

The first prototype tested the underlying Three Circles model as a simple web experience.

We are now building the first playable version of **无聊么？ / Bored?**

The first chapter focuses on one small complete loop:

🏠 Home  
↓  
🚪 Explore  
↓  
✨ Strange event  
↓  
🌿 Discover a clue  
↓  
🗺️ Map changes  
↓  
🏝️ Hidden island appears  
↓  
🥚 Something is waiting there...

The first milestone is intentionally small:

> **Can we make someone play for ten minutes and genuinely want to know what happens next?**
